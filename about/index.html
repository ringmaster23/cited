Methodology

How claims get into the library

Every sentence on this site is a timestamped clip from a named conversation. Click to hear it.

How a claim is sourced

The Cited Conversations podcast records structured expert conversations on defined topics. After each recording, the episode is transcribed and reviewed. Individual exchanges are extracted as standalone answer pages — one question, one answer, one speaker, one timestamp link.

Each answer page carries:

  • The speaker’s name, title, and company
  • The show name and episode number
  • The exact timestamp where the answer begins
  • A link to that moment in the source recording, playable directly

How attribution works

Every claim is attributed to a named speaker. Speakers are confirmed at intake by the show’s producer — we record who they are, their role, and the organization they represent before the conversation begins. Attribution is not inferred from the transcript; it is established before recording starts.

Speaker entity data (name, title, organization, LinkedIn profile) is embedded in the page’s structured markup so AI answer engines can resolve the attribution without parsing prose.

How verification works

Every claim links to a playable moment in the source recording. If the link doesn’t work, the claim doesn’t ship.

Before a page publishes, a human reviewer checks that:

  • The written answer accurately represents what the speaker said
  • The attributed speaker is the person who said it
  • The timestamp link opens at the correct moment in the episode

We spot-audit published pages against source audio on an ongoing basis.

What does not get in

The library is deliberately narrow. We exclude:

  • AI-generated conversations or AI-written answers
  • Solo commentary segments without a named guest
  • Re-aired or recycled episodes that weren’t freshly recorded
  • Any claim we cannot link to a playable moment in a source recording

The constraint is intentional. An unlinked claim is an unverifiable claim. If we cannot play it, it doesn’t belong here.

The underlying principle

AI answer engines are increasingly the first place people get answers to professional questions. Those engines pull from whatever text they can find. Most of that text is uncited, undated, and unspeakered.

Cited Conversations is built on the opposite assumption: that the most useful thing we can do is make source attribution machine-readable. Every answer here is a piece of structured evidence, not a piece of content.
