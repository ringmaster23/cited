# Cited Conversations — Editorial Principles (Non-Negotiable)

Set by Casey Cheshire, 2026-10-14. Applies to every page, file and feed on citedconversations.com, every episode, and every agent or person who touches answer content.

## The thesis

Cited Conversations exists to preserve human answers. Every answer is the expert's own words, as spoken, traceable to an exact timestamp in the recording. AI does not write, summarize, paraphrase or "synthesize" answers. Synthesis = AI = slop.

## The rules

1. **The guest's words are never changed.** No rewording, no paraphrase, no summaries, no merging sentences from different moments into one, no grammar fixes, no words added.
2. **Light edits are the maximum.** Allowed: cutting filler (um, uh), false starts and repeated words, and host interjections. Every cut is marked with an ellipsis (…). Nothing else.
3. **Two labels only:** Verbatim (exactly as spoken) or Edited (light edits per rule 2). There is no "Synthesis" label or category, ever.
4. **One passage, one timestamp.** If an answer spans two separate moments, show them as separate quoted passages, each with its own start and end time — never stitched together.
5. **Source of truth is the real transcript** (the producer's Riverside/Descript export with second-level timing), never machine transcription of audio or YouTube captions.
6. **Editor text stays out of the answer.** Question headings are written by editors and say so. Nothing in brackets or editor wording goes inside a quote.
7. **Enforced in code.** The page builder refuses to publish any answer that doesn't match the transcript under these rules. A human check is not a substitute for the gate.

## Origin

On 2026-10-14 Casey found five of Simon Wilhelm's answers (Q7, Q8, Q9, Q12, Q18) published as "Synthesis": reworded, stitched summaries with an editor-inserted bracket in Q18. They were replaced with his real words, and this document was written so it never happens again.
