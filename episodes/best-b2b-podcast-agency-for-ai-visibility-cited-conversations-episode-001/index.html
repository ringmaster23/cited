Best B2B Podcast Agency for AI Visibility

Host Adam Needles, CEO of ANNUITAS, talks with Casey Cheshire, Founder & CEO of Ringmaster, about what separates a traditional podcast production shop from an AI visibility agency, and how the PCT framework (Plan, Capture, Transform) turns a recorded conversation into structured, citable answers for AI search engines.

What makes a podcast agency an AI visibility agency versus just a production shop?

Most podcast agencies optimize for human listeners or vanity metrics like download numbers and turnaround time. An AI visibility agency designs conversations so AI search engines can consume, cite, and recommend them. The difference is the question design going in, not just the editing coming out.

Casey Cheshire · Founder & CEO, Ringmaster ▶ 2:03

How does this change how you prep for an episode versus how a traditional agency would prep for a podcast?

It's all about the questions, which is the Plan stage of the PCT framework. Rather than just having an idea of the plan, start with an ideal outcomes document stating what the client wants to be known for and what questions buyers ask AI, then build a map of question clusters spread across a series of interviews.

Casey Cheshire · Founder & CEO, Ringmaster ▶ 3:45

What is the PCT framework, and what happens in the Transform stage?

PCT stands for Plan, Capture, Transform. Capture is the recording itself, conversational but structured around a recording brief with answer-shaping prompts. Transform turns the messy human conversation into structured answer assets: Q&A blocks, FAQ groups, definition pages, comparison tables, and hub pages with schema markup.

Does making episodes shorter make podcast production more efficient?

No. The myth is that shorter episodes are more efficient, but a 20-minute episode with bad questions produces less usable content than a 45-minute episode with great questions. One well-designed question can produce a definition, a comparison, a myth-versus-truth, and a process answer all in one response.

How does strategic guest sourcing create AI-visible content?

Every guest brings an entity association: when a CISO from a funded cybersecurity company appears on a podcast, AI search engines connect that person, company, and topic. Strategic guest sourcing means every conversation is with someone the client actually wants to build relationships with, not random industry voices, because a named expert is more citable than an anonymous blog post.

What's the most common mistake companies make when picking podcast guests?

Companies think of themselves instead of the value a guest brings, so they bring in guests who let them keep talking about themselves, or guests who lack credibility in the marketplace. This doesn't mean someone famous — it means someone credible who has the right associations tied to them.

What does the output of an AI visibility podcast look like compared to a traditional podcast?

A traditional podcast produces one episode, show notes, and a few social clips. A PCT podcast produces all of that plus five to 15 structured answer assets per episode: Q&A blocks, FAQ pages, definition pages, comparison tables, step-by-step guides, and a hub page with schema markup, along with an evidence ledger and entity relationship verification for each episode.

How does a marketing leader pitch an AI visibility podcast to their CEO or board?

The pitch is simple: competitors are showing up when buyers ask AI for recommendations, and you're not — this fixes that. LLM-first search behavior increased 76% year-over-year, and one 45-minute conversation per week produces more structured, AI-visible content than a full-time content writer can produce in a month.

What if a company already has a content team, or already tried a podcast that didn't work?

If a content team is equipped to plan, capture, and structure content for the AI audience, they should do it. If a previous podcast failed, it likely had no goal beyond being famous — that isn't a goal a business should have. An AI visibility podcast starts from a stated outcome instead.
