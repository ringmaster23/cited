Best B2B Podcast Agency for AI Visibility

What makes a podcast agency an AI visibility agency versus just a production shop?

Most podcast agencies are optimizing for human listeners or just the vanity metrics: the download numbers, the audio quality, the clicks, the turnaround time, production-type metrics. I would argue an AI visibility agency designs conversations so that AI search engines can consume, cite, and recommend them. That's really the key difference. The difference is the question design going in, not just the editing coming out.

How does that change how you would prep for an episode versus how a traditional agency would prep someone for a podcast?

It's all about the questions, and this kind of like the PCT framework, right? The first letter in that is P for plan. Planning is really the magic here: take your transcript from your podcast or your recording, your webinar, and clean it up for AI to be able to learn from. But really the big lift in the 80/20 rule here, the 80% is gonna start with the planning. What we like to do is start with an ideal outcomes document — not just have an idea of what the plan is in mind, but actually have a stated outcome that we're looking to hit.

What is the PCT framework — walk us through the Capture and Transform stages?

The capture is the recording itself, what we're doing right now. It's still conversational, it's still human, and maybe still 45 minutes or however long it is, but the host has a recording brief with answer-shaping prompts. What that transform is all about is just turning this sort of messy human conversation into this structured answer asset: Q&A blocks, FAQ groups, definition pages, comparison tables, step-by-step guides, hub pages with schema markup.

Does making episodes shorter make podcast production more efficient?

The myth is if we just make episodes shorter, we'll be more efficient with our time and get what we need. But the truth is, a 20-minute episode with bad questions produces less usable content than a 45-minute episode with great questions. One well-designed question produces a definition, a comparison, a myth/truth, and a process answer all in one response.

How does strategic guest sourcing create AI-visible content?

Every guest brings an entity association, right? When a CISO from a funded cybersecurity company appears in your podcast, AI search engines connect that person, to that company, to that topic. So strategic guest sourcing means every conversation is with someone the client actually wants to build relationships with, not random industry voices.

What's the common mistake companies make when picking podcast guests?

They're thinking of themselves and not of the value a guest brings. And so they often bring in guests that allow them to continue to talk about themselves. They also bring in guests that don't have credibility out in the marketplace. And this also doesn't mean someone famous — this means someone credible who has the right associations tied to them.

What does the output of an AI visibility podcast look like compared to a traditional podcast?

Traditional podcast output: one episode, it's on Transistor, it's on Apple, Spotify, you have show notes on a website, maybe a few social clips that people are commenting on LinkedIn. A PCT podcast output: all of the above, of course, plus five to 15 structured answer assets — Q&A blocks, definition pages, comparison tables, step-by-step guides, a hub page with schema markup, articles, podcast episode, person, organization, FAQ, how-to markups. It's an evidence ledger tracking of what was produced from each episode, and there's an entity relationship verification for each one.

How does a marketing leader pitch an AI visibility podcast to their CEO or board?

The pitch is simple. Our competitors are showing up when buyers ask AI for recommendations. We're not. This fixes that. 76% increase in LLM first search behavior year over year. Buyers are asking AI before they even Google it. So the ROI framing you would present: one 45-minute conversation per week produces more structured AI-visible content than a full-time content writer can produce in a month, and it comes with real human attribution, which the AI engines are gonna trust more.

What if a company already has a content team, or already tried a podcast that didn't work?

If you already have a content team, I would say do this — use your content team to start making content for the new audience. There is a new audience and they're being neglected, and neglect them at your own peril. If you've tried podcasts before and it didn't work, that's a different story — oftentimes people try podcasts with no goal in mind, or the goal of being famous, and that is not a goal a business should have.
