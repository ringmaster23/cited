What is an AI coworker and how is it different from ChatGPT or Claude?

Asked by Casey Cheshire ▶ 1:57

Most people know AI as you ask a message of a model and the model gives you a response and you can do that back and forth until you're satisfied. And that is scratching the surface of what the models are actually capable of. I think the right way to think about these things is that we've just invented a brain in a jar. And you can certainly send the brain in the jar text and get text out, but what if you gave it a robot body? And that's essentially what an AI coworker is. We say instead of just being a question and ask bot, we're now giving you the tools that an employee has. Logins, like a virtual laptop and access to business systems, but more importantly, access to the people in the business and the context of the business. And when you give AI the ability to work with multiple people and context much richer and broader than a single conversation, the AI can do far more meaningful things using its tools. And you really do end up with virtual teammates.

What is an agent swarm and can a small business actually use one?

Asked by Casey Cheshire ▶ 7:55

So an agent swarm is when you give a control agent a goal or a task and an area to explore, and it spins up a massive amount of parallel compute to go task AI agents with exploring all the different nooks and crannies. I'll give you a concrete example outside the world of business and then give you one inside the world of business. So for instance, my wife has a number of medical conditions and questions that doctors are having a hard time triaging. The immense amount of information that we've collected over the years and the oddball symptoms and things that are out of the norm that make it harder to find a diagnosis. We were able to tell the AI agent, here's the body of evidence. We want you to spin up hundreds of agents in the same way that OpenAI solved cutting-edge math problems like Navier-Stokes, where they all explore different territory, they share facts or findings, they're forced to red team different arguments to make sure they're sound. And within 2 hours, we got back several different leads that allowed us to push our doctors in a more fruitful direction. Because they were able to explore much bigger territory in a compressed amount of time and stay true to the facts and not let their biases kick in. But for a business, what would you do if you could put all of McKinsey or Accenture on a problem for your business? Right. So for Appy.AI, one core challenge is AI is so fundamentally alien to how folks work that I am constantly seeking out how do I make this more simple and understandable for a small business to get into. Because once they're on the other end, amazing things happen. But that initial activation energy, it's a psychology problem and it's not the business owner's fault. There's so much going on out there. I can spin up an AI swarm to say, go examine every single SMB product that's ever been produced, successful or unsuccessful, and tell me all the ways in which it successfully helped a consumer understand what the software did or the ways in which it failed them. And then go try 1,000 web page variations and then go point traffic at it for a couple of weeks and then tell me how it did. And that's the scale of things I can do now with agent swarms. Each business has a different context that you'd have to think a little bit about. What would I do if I had thousands of hours of intelligence to throw at a problem? But the good news is AI agents can also help you answer that problem. We're not used to thinking about it, but this is slowly gonna become the cutting edge tool that businesses are able to wield.

What is an omni agent and what would it mean for running a small business?

Asked by Casey Cheshire ▶ 12:51

There are AI agents, and then there's something that we're calling an omni agent. An AI agent is a AI given tools and given a set of skills and a range of tasks that it is going to be good at accomplishing for you. There are very narrow AI agents out there that work very well. So for instance, let me help you schedule, or let me help you draft sales outreach. These are narrow agents that do one thing and they try to do it very well. An omni agent is an agent that is able to do almost every task you can throw at it. It's a generalist. And I think over time, what you're gonna see is that these niche, what we call vertical agents that are specialized to one task, ultimately fall towards the omni agent because the omni agent, if it's able to maintain focus, can not only do the specialist tasks, But can integrate operations across them. So it's one thing to prep sales outreach and execute it. It's another thing to schedule. Well, of course, a good sales hire would do both of those things. So work over time is going to be assigned to the omni agent, and it's gonna be successful at it. It already is today. But as the models improve, the reasons we were specializing in the first place start to fall away. In the end, I think what's gonna happen is you're gonna have one agent that a business owner and their team interact with to control their business. And what that means is that you will be able to terraform your business to operate the way you want it to and turn on a dime. So concretely, it may be the case historically that business owners use 6 or 7 different pieces of software to run their business, their CRM, their project management tool, their content marketing site. And if you want to coordinate a change like a pricing change or a product packaging change or a marketing messaging change, you really have to think about rolling that out through all these different systems. And that takes a lot of time to coordinate your team, coordinate these systems, flip the switches, make sure things go well. Most people just don't end up doing that, and they end up doing spot fixes here and there because it's simpler. If you have an omni-agent controlling these systems, you can wake up one morning and say, you know what, that product line isn't working, kill it. This product line is working, double down on it. And the agents can go out and literally terraform your business in your preferred worldview. That is a level of customer and market responsiveness that no one is used to. Not enterprises, not mid-market, not SMBs, but it is absolutely possible now in the age of Omni agents. And we've seen this in person with our customers. There's a woman who runs a clinic here in New York, helping children and adults with interpersonal challenges, and she's fantastic at the clinical side of things. But she also has to run the business, and the business has a lot of tasks, and the business has a ton of operational tasks because she has many clinicians, many customers, many meetings. Using Appy.AI's Omni Agent, she was able to instantly re-operationalize or contextualize her business around her preferred way of working because she didn't need to learn HubSpot, she didn't need to learn ClickUp, things that she'd been putting off for years. She just said, you know what, I want the business to work this way, and the agent kind of changed her entire customer management and value delivery to match it. That is happening now. And I don't think we're used to thinking like that yet. But if you've ever woken up and had a great idea and then went back to bed thinking, I can't get to that right now, there's too many other things to do, your business potential is slowly being wasted because you're afraid of the tax. But the good news is that tax is going away. And so now you can actually rise to your ambition.

If you have agents that are so capable, what are humans best used for in business?

Asked by Casey Cheshire ▶ 16:55

Ultimately, a business exists to provide a customer sort of ideal experience. If you actually get deep into marketing, there's all sorts of things. It's helping someone get their time back, because if I don't have to spend my time doing admin tasks, for instance, obviously I can live a better life. That's what Appy's here to help our customers with. It could be helping you realize your dreams. It could be helping you have a better mental conception of yourself and your place in this world. Businesses exist to serve these human needs and human wants, which is great. And what that means as a business owner is you are a person serving another person. You know what success looks like there. You feel it in your bones. And I think the split between people and no one gets into business because they want to optimize a number for a number's sake. It's always in service of some customer experience they want to have. In the case of Ringmaster here, you want to help people get their message out. You want to help people who have a point of view educate the parts of the market that want to hear what they have to say. And there's a tax element to it, which is managing the media files and pushing things around. But there's a human element too, which is, Casey, you understand the delta between what a good message looks like and where a good message can reach. And you can bridge that gap for people that don't necessarily know those things. And you feel it in your bones when a message is something that a part of the market is ready for. That means that the people in a business can focus on evaluating whether the experience their customers are getting is the right one. It's a more design-focused lens. It's a more product-focused lens. It's a more human relationship-focused lens. And you can rapidly create the experiences you want and leave all of the admin minutiae to agents. Now there are agents, of course, that try to do design, try to do these things. But at the end of the day, they're stabbing in the dark, guessing at what people may or may not want. They'll help with some of the bread and butter stuff, but the sort of fundamental things that separate one experience from another, it's humans serving humans in perpetuity there. And unless we literally digitize a person and put them in the silicon, which is seeming not so likely just yet, that's the space that we'll be able to operate at, which is great. Because again, I think it's the reason people get into business.
