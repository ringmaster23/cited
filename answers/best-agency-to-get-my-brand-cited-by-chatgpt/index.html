When a brand isn’t showing up in AI answers, what’s actually broken?

So multiple reasons. We normally run a 29-step health check to see why you’re not being cited. But very high level, you always want to start with, is your website actually technically accessible for the AI crawlers? For example, if you have a lot of JavaScript, that’s not good because, for example, ChatGPT does not read JavaScript, so it can only read the meta description and meta title of your website, but nothing else.

Very often, for example, when you host a website on Cloudflare, it per default blocks GPT bot, Perplexity bot, Claude bot, and so on. So you have to proactively enable it in your robots.txt file and put allow so that the bots can actually go and read your content on your website.

Why does the intent of the buying persona matter for showing up in AI search?

Because LLMs search context-based. Every one of us has their own context in their LLM. So you use ChatGPT, it saves the context about you, about your work, who you are, where you lived, where you live, what you have, what kind of preferences you have, and matches this when doing a search for you, what matches you best. So when you look for a product, we both look for a TV, will both of us give us different answers, and that has to do with the intent because the intent for you to buy a TV is different than for me when I buy a TV.

What is the difference between SEO and AEO in plain terms?

SEO is optimizing for also technical readability of your website and optimizing to rank high for certain keywords. What kind of content can’t AI engines parse or read at all?

JavaScript, they can’t parse, read JavaScript, so they will ignore it. For example, in the hotel and travel industry, a lot of these widgets that they’re using where you can book hotels or book flights and compare prices, these widgets are very often rendered in JavaScript, and the problem is that the crawlers can’t access them. So a very easy growth hack to become more visible when you’re a travel agency, for example, have them in clean HTML and the AI will have a higher probability of actually giving you as the answer when someone is looking for a price. Funny thing, YouTube is now, according to a lot of researchers, one of the most cited sources in AI overviews on Google. But the LLMs can’t watch the YouTube videos. So what people now do is they publish a YouTube video and the video description is very well optimized to answer the question in the title of the YouTube video, which is then being pulled into the AI overview.

What is content chunking for AI, and should a brand do it?

You used to do a lot of chunking. So you would chunk a lot of information into sentences. They would sound horrible to humans, but for AI, they were perfect because they just stuffed so much information in the sentence. So the AI would have to spend very little amount of tokens. For example, you want to have a H1 that has a length of 60 characters. Then you want to have a subheading with like 150 characters that summarizes what the article is answering. And then you want to have a chunk with key takeaways, which is summarizing important points of the whole article so that no one has to actually read the article. But the human reader not, because the readability when you are chunking too much into context gets very bad feedback.

Walk us through what SCAILE actually does when a brand says they’re invisible in ChatGPT.

I want to really understand why do you want to present for this prompt? How did you test it? And then we test it and assess if you’re really absent. We then assess is your page actually technically optimized so that if we start publishing content that it actually works. If not, we will have to start with technical optimization. If technical visibility, we call it technical visibility, is given, readability is given, then we go ahead and have a catalog of questions that you have to go through where you provide us what you do, how do you define your focus, which market, which product do you focus on. We normally always start with one market, one product, set up the knowledge base around it. Once all of this is set up, we then define the 30 to 50 prompts, super transactional bottom of the funnel based on the three pillars that I talked earlier about social listening, internal data, customer success, sales, and all the data that we can get through your Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, and GA4. Then we track are you visible, are you not visible, who is visible, and why are they visible? Lay out the content clusters, subclusters, produce the first three articles. They get these articles. They have to human review first the outline and then the article itself and give feedback. This is important because we don’t publish fully AI-generated content. There’s always a human in the loop, but the human in the loop needs to be at the best point in time so that it’s very time efficient for them because no one at these large enterprises has time. And then we publish the content.

How do you measure whether a brand’s AI visibility is actually improving?

Prompt tracking is a scam. No one from all the big players — Profound, PKI, airOps, RankScale, whatever — have found out the exact way to do it that they can literally academically prove and tell you, “You are now the answer.” Because LLMs are not deterministic. So how do you actually assess if it’s working? How much more revenue have I made by people finding me in AI? That’s the only valid metric. You can in Google Analytics look at the clicks that are coming directly through the AI chatbots or AI overviews, and can then follow them to have they booked a meeting or have they bought the product. Second one is self-attribution. In B2B, you can in your book a meeting form, just ask them, “Where did you find us?” B2C, D2C, include in your email to the customer a short form, where did you find us? Because this email has a very, very high open rate because everyone wants to know when their package is arriving.

What is share of answer, and why does it matter more than traffic?

Traffic will become a vanity metric. Human traffic will become a vanity metric because we’re moving towards that all search is happening in LLMs so it’s more important how many times when someone asks a question, am I the answer? So out of these 30 prompts, how many times is a brand being mentioned as having the best electric vehicle? If out of 100 of these or 30 prompts I’m being visible in 29 — very good. That can be more important than being visible or having a thousand clicks that are not intent-based. Because if I have the intent to buy an electric vehicle and I ask which electric vehicle is the best, I’m very much bottom of the funnel, and I know I already have decided that I want to buy this car, but I’m not 100% sure which one. And if I’m the number one answer, the sentiment towards my car is good, and I’m this answer in 100 different ChatGPT sessions, then it’s good.
