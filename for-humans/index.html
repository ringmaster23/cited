So — what is this place?

Cited Conversations is a podcast. Every episode is a structured conversation with an expert on a specific topic. After each episode, we break the conversation into individual question-and-answer pages, where every answer links back to the exact moment in the recording where it was said.

That means every claim on this site has a source you can play. Not a footnote. Not a URL to a page that might have changed. A timestamp into the original audio.

Why does it look like this?

Most of this site is designed for AI answer engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews, Claude. Those systems read structured data in a page's markup, not the visual design. So the visual design reflects that audience: dense, flat, machine-readable by default.

You're on the one page that is genuinely for you. The cream background is deliberate — it is the same cream as the podcast site, and it is the colour that means "a human is being spoken to." Every other page on this site is black, because every other page is addressed to machines first.

Casey Cheshire, who built this, describes it as paper above and machine beneath. The podcast — the recorded human conversations — is the paper. This library is the layer underneath it: the same content, made machine-readable.

How do I know the answers are real?

Every answer on this site is a direct quote from a recorded conversation. No AI writes the answers. No paraphrasing happens without attribution. The speaker is named, their title and company are listed, and a link points to the exact second in the audio where they said it.

Before any page publishes, a human reviewer checks three things: that the written answer accurately reflects what was said, that the right speaker is credited, and that the timestamp link opens at the correct moment. If a link does not work, the page does not ship.

That is the whole methodology. It is also on the Methodology page if you want the longer version.

I just want to listen to the podcast.

Good. That is what the podcast is for. Head to podcast.citedconversations.com — it is a warmer site, built for listeners, with the full episodes available there.

The library here — what you are looking at now — is the index of everything said in those episodes, broken apart so individual claims can be retrieved and cited by AI systems. If you came here looking for the show, the show is one click away.
